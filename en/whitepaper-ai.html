Love or Fear?

The two drivers behind AI adoption — and why one always wins

Amir Bercovitz  ·  byamir.ai

Foreword

Something is in the air.

You feel it. In conversations with colleagues, in the news, in the eyes of a competitor who is suddenly moving faster. Artificial Intelligence is everywhere — and the pressure to do something with it grows by the day.

But what drives you? What truly drives your organisation when you think about AI?

In twenty years of commercial leadership — at Sodexo, where AI adoption in an international organisation of over €2.4 billion was not theory but daily reality — I saw two patterns returning again and again. Leaders who asked: what can this mean for us? And leaders who asked: what happens to us if we do nothing? Those two questions lead to different behaviour, different decisions and radically different outcomes.

From practice

Two department heads. The same AI tool. The same training. Six months later: one team had accelerated three processes and gained two new client insights. The other team had installed the tool and barely used it. The difference was not in the technology. It was in the question they started with. The first asked: what can we do with this? The second asked: what do we now have to do with this?

"The greatest threat of AI is the fear of AI. The greatest opportunity of AI is the love of AI."

— Amir Bercovitz

This whitepaper is not a technical manual. It is an invitation to consciously choose which driver sits at the wheel — before AI does it for you.

01

The race nobody announced

Somewhere in 2023, something shifted. ChatGPT reached a hundred million users in two months. Suddenly everyone had an opinion on AI — and suddenly every business leader who had not yet acted felt left behind.

That feeling is understandable. But there is a difference between urgency and panic. Urgency leads to clear choices. Panic leads to rushed implementations, tools nobody uses, and investments that deliver nothing.

For an organisation of fifty people that delays AI adoption for two years while competitors embrace it: that is two years of productivity gap, two years of recruitment disadvantage, two years of momentum left untapped. The cost of inaction is real — but it justifies urgency, not panic.

Driven by fear
Reactive
  • Competitor uses AI → so must we
  • Buy the tool, skip the vision
  • Team follows without conviction
  • Result: chaos, resistance
  • AI as obligation
Driven by purpose
Intentional
  • Ambition defines → AI supports
  • Vision first, tool follows
  • Team understands the why
  • Result: adoption, value
  • AI as an ally

The organisations achieving real results with AI today are not the earliest adopters. They are the most intentional ones.

02

Master & Servant — a framework that works

In my work I use a simple framework: is AI the Master or the Servant in your organisation?

When AI is the Master, your organisation chases the technology. You adapt your processes to the tool. The tool dictates the direction. When AI is the Servant, you set the direction — and use AI as a lever. The technology adapts to your ambitions.

The difference between Master and Servant is not a technical question. It is a question of leadership. Who determines the course in your organisation — you, or the hype?

Fear automatically makes AI a Master. Because you are reacting to external pressure, you give away control. You participate in a race you did not start.

Purpose — for your organisation, for your people, for your clients — makes AI a Servant. You choose deliberately. You implement with intent. You keep the wheel.

03

Five dimensions, two lenses

AI readiness has five dimensions. In each, you can see the difference between fear and purpose as a driver.

Strategy
Fear: AI on the agenda because everyone is talking about it. No clear goal.
Purpose: AI as an extension of an existing growth ambition. Goal determines tool.
A production company of 40 people that automates its quoting process driven by "how do we win more contracts?" is operating from purpose. One that does it because a competitor has it too is operating from fear.
Data
Fear: Collecting data because it "must be done". Fragmented, unmanaged.
Purpose: Data as strategic capital. Consciously managed, deployed with intent.
The question is not: how much data do you have? The question is: which data helps you make the decisions that truly matter?
People
Fear: Team experiences AI as a threat to their jobs. Resistance as the result.
Purpose: Team experiences AI as an enhancement of their work. Ownership as the result.
The difference lies in how the introduction happens. "This will make your work easier" is a different message than "we need to be more efficient."
Processes
Fear: Deploying AI on poorly understood processes. Automating chaos.
Purpose: Understanding processes first, then reinforcing them. AI as a precision instrument.
Culture
Fear: Leadership moves reactively. No trust, no direction.
Purpose: Leadership chooses deliberately. Trust as the foundation for adoption.
04

The deliberate choice

Choosing deliberately for AI from a place of purpose begins with one question, before you acquire a single tool, launch a single project, or free up a single budget:

What problem do I want to solve — and for whom?

Most AI projects stall right here: there is a tool, a budget, a timeline — but no owner of the problem. When you work from purpose, you start with your people. Which tasks drain their energy without adding value? Where does your organisation lose time, quality, speed? Those are your starting points for AI.

Three questions for intentional AI adoption:
1. What concrete problem are we solving — and who is affected by it?
2. How will we know it worked — what is the measurable result?
3. Who owns this in our organisation — and has the mandate?

05

Choose your driver

AI will not save you. AI will not ruin you either. AI is an instrument — powerful, versatile, fast. But an instrument without direction is dangerous.

The organisations that look back five years from now on a successful AI journey are not the ones that started earliest. They are the ones that started most intentionally.

That choice begins today — in how you talk about AI in your organisation, in the questions you ask, in the projects you do and do not start.

"The greatest threat of AI is the fear of AI. The greatest opportunity of AI is the love of AI."

— Amir Bercovitz

Choose your driver. The rest follows.
