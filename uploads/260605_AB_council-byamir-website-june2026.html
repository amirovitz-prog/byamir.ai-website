Website Assessment — byamir.ai

Generated · June 2026 · 7 advisors · peer review · chairman · spiritual advisor
Framed question: Is www.byamir.ai — as currently built and written — effective as a commercial website for a new independent AI consulting practice targeting Dutch mid-market CEOs and Commercial Directors? Does it generate sufficient confidence and urgency to convert a visitor into a booked call?
Spiritual Advisor — Opening
The question brought forward is "does this website work?" — but the soul already suspects the deeper question: "Am I visible enough to be trusted, or still hiding behind craft?" There is something beautiful and something incomplete here. A man who carries the philosophy of love over fear has built a container that is clean and serious — but a container without warmth is just a vessel. The greatest opportunity is not in the words or the layout. It is in whether the person behind the brand can be felt. What would this site look like if its creator were not afraid of being truly seen?

Chairman's Verdict

Where the council agrees

  • The site is professionally built, visually on-brand, and correctly positioned. It does not embarrass. For warm contacts who Google Amir after a conversation, it validates and reinforces.
  • The positioning logic ("niet tech-leverancier, maar commercieel leider") is exactly right and consistently executed across pages.
  • The 4-step werkwijze (Kennismaking → Assessment → Debrief → Traject) is the best structural element on the site — it reduces buyer anxiety and sets expectations clearly.
  • The absence of pricing is the single biggest conversion inhibitor. Every advisor flagged it independently.
  • The "Over Amir" page undersells the most persuasive credential: 10–15% new business growth on a €2.4B portfolio. That number is not on the site. It should be the first thing a buyer reads.

Where the council clashes

Pricing transparency: The Financial Realist and Executor say publish the assessment price now (€5,500 fixed). The Long-View Steward cautions this might commoditize and trigger price-shoppers before Amir has established enough authority. Resolution: the Long-View Steward is partially right but wrong on timing. At launch, with zero case studies, published pricing signals confidence and removes friction. Revisit in 6 months when social proof exists.

Three pillars vs. one: The Contrarian says three pillars confuses buyers who don't know which door to walk through. The Expansionist says the breadth signals rare seniority. Resolution: the Contrarian wins on the homepage. One clear primary action ("Start with an AI Readiness Scan" or equivalent) must dominate. The three pillars can live on the diensten page.

Blind spots the council caught

  • The master quote — "De grootste bedreiging van AI is de angst voor AI. De grootste kans is de liefde voor AI." — appears buried mid-page in a decorative block. This is the philosophical core of the brand. It should be in the hero, not hidden below the fold.
  • No social proof of any kind. No testimonials, no client names (even anonymized sectors), no case studies. For a new practice, even one sentence from a former colleague about working style would carry weight. This is a structural gap, not a copy gap.
  • The EN language version — is it live? If it is, it must be equally strong. If it's not ready, remove the toggle. A broken or thin EN page sent to an international contact undermines credibility instantly.
  • The LinkedIn link in the footer goes to a URL that should be verified. More importantly: LinkedIn should be in the hero or nav — not just the footer. It is currently the only social proof vector and it's buried.
  • No clear primary CTA hierarchy. "Plan een vrijblijvend gesprek" and "Bekijk de diensten" compete equally. One must win. The call-to-action architecture is flat.

The recommendation

The site is a solid foundation. It is not broken. But it is built for a consultant who already has a reputation — not for someone building one from scratch. The gap between what the site shows and what the buyer needs to feel confident enough to call is currently bridged by Amir's conversations, not by the site itself. That is acceptable for warm network outreach today. It will become a bottleneck when LinkedIn starts generating cold or warm-cold traffic. Fix the five things below before that traffic arrives.

One thing to do first: Add the €2.4B / 10–15% growth figure to the homepage hero or Over Amir intro — one sentence. Then add the assessment fixed price to the diensten page. These two changes take 30 minutes and immediately close the two biggest confidence gaps a buyer encounters before picking up the phone.

1. The Contrarian Concerns

The site is a beautifully designed business card that fails at its primary commercial job. Three structural flaws:

Flaw 1 — No proof, only claims. "20+ jaar bewezen resultaten" is stated four times across the site. Not once is a buyer given a number that makes that real. The CV has it: 10–15% new business growth on a €2.4B portfolio. 50% improvement in deal conversion. 35% improvement in bid win ratio at Facilicom. None of these appear on the website. Instead we get "+50% dealconversie verbeterd" on the Over Amir page — without context. Improved whose deal conversion? By when? Under what conditions? Claims without context are noise.

Flaw 2 — Three pillars, no entry point. A mid-market CEO lands on the homepage. He sees AI Consultancy, Growth Leadership, Bid Strategy. All three feel relevant. He clicks "Bekijk de diensten" and finds 14 service lines. No anchor. No recommended starting point that is visually distinct. The "★ Startpunt" label on each pillar is too small and too passive. The buyer exits without calling because he couldn't figure out which door was his.

Flaw 3 — The philosophy is decorative, not functional. The love-vs-fear quote appears once, below the fold, in a decorative pull-quote block. This is the differentiating worldview of the entire practice. A buyer who responds to that philosophy would feel it immediately if it were in the hero. Currently it is invisible to anyone who doesn't scroll past three full sections. The philosophy is being treated as a slogan, not as a commercial argument.

None of these are fatal. All are fixable in under a week. The question is whether the urgency is felt yet.

2. The First Principles Thinker Mixed

Strip away assumptions. What is this website actually for?

In the first 90 days, this website serves one population: warm contacts who met Amir in person or through referral, want to validate him before calling back, and spend 60–90 seconds on the site before making that decision. For that use case, the site already works. It is professional, consistent, and signals seniority. It will not lose a warm contact.

The problem is what the site is being built to become: a conversion engine for LinkedIn-driven warm-cold traffic. That visitor arrives with no prior Amir context, 15–30 seconds of attention, and a simple question: "Is this person credible enough for me to spend 30 minutes of my time?" That visitor needs three things the site currently doesn't provide efficiently: (1) one overwhelming credential in the first scroll, (2) a clear price signal that confirms this is serious and appropriately priced, (3) a reason to act now rather than later.

The site is designed for consultation, not conversion. It explains the model, describes the services, names the values. But it doesn't create urgency. "Klaar om de kloof te overbruggen?" is a passive question. It assumes the buyer already agrees. What would make a buyer who is on the fence feel urgency? Scarcity (limited capacity), social proof (others already moving), or a sharp insight that reframes their problem. None of these exist yet.

The fundamental question this site doesn't answer: why now? Why is this the moment to call by AMIR, this week, rather than in three months?

Peer review received
The Executor flags this as actionable: urgency can be added without changing the site structure — a "currently available for 2 new clients" signal in the CTA, or a seasonal anchor. The Long-View Steward warns against manufactured scarcity: it conflicts with the love-not-fear brand. Consensus: authentic urgency (real capacity constraint) beats manufactured urgency.
3. The Expansionist Positive

Most advisors will focus on what's missing. I want to name what's being undervalued, because the upside here is larger than it looks.

The love-vs-fear philosophy is a content engine that isn't being used. That quote — "De grootste bedreiging van AI is de angst voor AI. De grootste kans is de liefde voor AI." — is not a tagline. It is a point of view that no competitor in the Dutch mid-market AI consulting space has staked out. If that philosophy were the organizing principle of a blog, a LinkedIn content series, and a lead magnet (the e-book mentioned in the roadmap), the site would generate inbound rather than just validating outbound. Right now the site has no content, no lead magnet, no reason to return. It is a brochure. It could be a destination.

The "By Amir. By AI." transparency principle is commercially underused. Amir is the only AI consultant in the Dutch market who is openly co-creating with AI as a brand statement. This is not a footnote — it is a proof point. A page or section called "How we work" that shows the AI-Meester/Dienaar relationship in action — with real examples — would be more persuasive than any service description. Clients want to see the methodology, not just the outputs.

The NL/EN toggle suggests international ambition. If that's real, the English version needs to be equally strong and should be positioned for EMEA enterprise contacts. The potential revenue from one enterprise EMEA client far exceeds several Dutch mid-market assessments.

The site is a strong platform. The question is when it graduates from brochure to engine.

5. The Executor VETO FLAG

⚑ VETO FLAG: The contact page has no direct booking mechanism. A form + phone number is not a conversion path for a busy C-suite buyer in 2026. This is the highest-priority fix.

Ranked action list:

Priority 1 (today): Add Calendly or equivalent to the contact page. "Plan een gesprek" must lead to a calendar, not a form. This is a 2-hour task. Revenue impact: immediate.

Priority 2 (this week): Add assessment pricing to the diensten page. "€5.500 excl. BTW · Vaste prijs · 2–4 weken" under the AI Readiness Scan, Sales ROI Assessment, and Bid Effectiveness Assessment. Three lines of text. Removes the single biggest pre-call friction point.

Priority 3 (this week): Add the portfolio scale credential to the homepage hero or Over Amir intro. "Als VP bij Sodexo stuurde ik een commerciële groeistrategie aan op €2,4 miljard portfolioniveau — met consistente dubbele-cijfer groei." One sentence. Trust is built in seconds.

Priority 4 (next week): Redesign the homepage hero CTA hierarchy. One primary CTA (book a call or start an assessment), one secondary (learn more). Currently equal. Buyers need one door to be clearly open.

Priority 5 (before first LinkedIn post): Verify and strengthen the EN language version. If it's thin or incomplete, disable the toggle until it's ready.

These five changes require no redesign, no new copy strategy, no agency. They are execution tasks. Do them before driving traffic.

6. The Financial Realist Concerns

Let's run the numbers on what this site's current gaps actually cost.

Revenue target: €15,000/month. Entry product: AI Readiness Scan at ~€5,500. To hit target via assessments alone requires ~2.7 assessments/month, some converting to retainers. Assume a retainer conversion rate of 30% (optimistic for a new practice), so roughly 2 assessments/month needed that convert, plus 1 retainer at €6–7K by month 4–5.

The bottleneck is not pricing or service quality. It is call-booking rate. How many visitors book a call? For a new consulting site with no social proof, industry benchmarks suggest 1–3% of unique visitors convert to a call booking. At 3%, you need 33–100 unique qualified visitors per month just to get 1–3 calls. Of those calls, a 40% close rate on assessments gives 0.4–1.2 assessments/month. That's below the revenue target.

What lifts conversion rate from 1–3% to 5–8%? Exactly what's missing: direct booking (Calendly lifts rate by estimated 30–50% in B2B services), visible pricing (removes pre-qualification friction for budget-conscious buyers), and social proof (single client testimonial can lift rate by 20–40%). These aren't design improvements — they are revenue improvements with measurable economic impact.

The "tarieven op aanvraag" policy on the diensten page is particularly costly. A buyer who doesn't know if they can afford the service will not book a call to find out. They'll move on. Publishing €5,500 for the entry assessment pre-qualifies buyers and actually improves call quality — buyers who call already know the price and have accepted it.

Peer review received
The Long-View Steward raises a counterpoint: publishing prices too early, before the brand is established, might anchor expectations at the lower end and make premium pricing harder later. The Financial Realist's response: that risk is real at scale; at launch with near-zero traffic, the risk of invisibility is far greater than the risk of price anchoring.
7. The Long-View Steward Mixed

Three years from now, what will matter about the decisions made on this site today?

What's being built correctly for the long view: The positioning — commercial leader who uses AI, not AI consultant — is a bet that will compound. As the market matures and AI becomes table stakes, Amir's differentiation as a senior commercial operator who happens to be fluent in AI will be more valuable, not less. The site correctly lays this foundation. Don't change this.

The book is invisible — and this is a missed compounding opportunity. The Handbook for World Domination, openly co-created with AI, is a once-in-a-decade brand asset in the making. It's not on the site. Not a reference, not a "coming soon" signal, not a mailing list for early readers. Every month that passes without a "coming soon" or lead magnet attached to the book is a month of potential audience compounding lost. The book and the consulting practice are not separate channels — they are the same brand, and the site should signal both.

The Meester/Dienaar philosophy is the long-term moat. No competitor will develop this worldview. But it only becomes a moat if it accumulates — in content, in client stories, in public thinking. The site currently holds this philosophy in a single pull-quote. In three years, it should be the organizing principle of a knowledge archive. Start that accumulation now, even with one piece of content per month.

One risk: If the EN toggle is live but the EN version is thin, and Amir starts attracting international attention through LinkedIn, the EN site will underperform the brand promise. Fix or hide it.
